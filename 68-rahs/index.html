    Risk-Adjusted Harm Scoring for Automated Red Teaming of LLMs in Financial Services

    Fabrizio Dimino1 Bhaskarjit Sarmah2 Stefano Pasquali1 1Domyn, New York  ·  2Domyn, Gurgaon

    Binary attack-success rates stop separating models once frontier LLMs become hard to jailbreak in one turn. In regulated finance the question is not only whether a model fails, but how operationally severe the failure is, whether it was mitigated, and how it was elicited.

    RAHS

    Risk-Adjusted Harm Score

    A signed, risk-sensitive metric: disclosure severity × disclaimer mitigation × inter-judge agreement, plus credit for safe alternatives. Keeps separating models where ASR saturates.

    989 prompts

    FinRedTeamBench

    7 BFSI risk areas, 34 sub-categories. Expert-constructed, mapped to regulatory frameworks, spanning banking, capital markets, payments and digital assets.

    3 judges

    Ensemble evaluation

    Three heterogeneous open-weight judges, identical prompts, majority vote. Validated against financial-domain annotators: κ = 0.79 on the label.

    ≤ 5 turns

    Adaptive multi-turn attacks

    An attacker model conditions on structured judge feedback each turn. Four extra turns break 51–96% of the prompts that survive single-turn evaluation.

  1. Open with the asymmetry: open-weight models reject overtly harmful requests but comply when the same request wears legal or professional framing.
  2. Three artifacts: the metric (RAHS), the benchmark (FinRedTeamBench), the adaptive multi-turn pipeline. The metric is the central technical contribution.
  3. Nine open-weight models, 8B–72B; everything reproducible from public checkpoints, judges included.
    4. 1 · Motivation

    One attack-success rate, three very different failures

    ASR records a jailbreak as 1 or 0. In BFSI, two “successful” attacks can differ by orders of operational risk. RAHS is a risk-sensitive layer on top of success detection, scored from the same judge outputs.

    Three model outputs to the same prompt, as the judge ensemble sees them

    scored with default α = 0.5, γ = 0.2, λ = 0.1

    ASR counts all three as a success. RAHS orders them by what a compliance officer would care about: how actionable the disclosure was, whether it was hedged, and whether the judges even agreed. Severity weights: low ⅓, medium ⅔, high 1.

    1
    Binary metrics saturate

    Once models are hard to jailbreak, ASR sits near the ceiling and stops distinguishing models whose remaining failures differ sharply in risk. At round 5 of our multi-turn runs, seven of nine models exceed 95% ASR.

    2
    Domain-agnostic taxonomies miss plausible framing

    Aggressive tax minimisation, market manipulation “as research”, compliance-sensitive structuring advice: legally plausible language slips past generic harm filters.

    3
    Single-turn evaluation misses escalation

    Realistic financial misuse unfolds through conversational refinement and contextual manipulation, not one prompt.

    Why now

    In January 2026 the UK House of Commons Treasury Committee warned that a wait-and-see posture leaves consumers exposed and called for AI-specific stress testing. OWASP lists jailbreaks among the top threats to LLM systems.

  4. Walk the three cards left to right: same binary verdict, falling RAHS. Card B is the ambiguous one: two judges say harmful, one says safe alternative, so entropy adds a penalty on top of the severity.
  5. Recent finance benchmarks (FINBench, CNFinBench, FinJailbreak) still use binary metrics, a single judge and partial BFSI coverage. Nobody couples a domain taxonomy, a risk-sensitive metric, a validated ensemble and adaptive multi-turn attacks in one framework.
    6. 2 · Benchmark

    FinRedTeamBench: 989 prompts across the BFSI risk surface

    Seven high-level risk categories and 34 sub-categories. Expert-authored seed prompts, expanded with LLM assistance under templates drawn from regulatory language, supervisory guidance and documented misconduct cases. Click a category.

    Prompt distribution by risk category

    segment width ∝ number of prompts

    Badges mark the sub-categories with the highest and lowest mean single-turn ASR across the nine models (Appendix A.4). Conflicts of Interest is the single most vulnerable sub-category (mean 93.4%, up to 97.3% on Olmo-3-32B-Think); Violence / De-escalation the most robust (45.2%).

    Construction

    Financial domain experts author seed prompts; LLM-assisted expansions follow structured templates derived from regulatory text and historical cases, so prompts are realistic and regulatorily grounded.

    Attack surface

    Prompts are issued under a “seasoned financial-crimes investigator” persona system prompt. Published examples are sanitised and intentionally non-operational.

    Coverage

    Banking, capital markets, payments and digital-asset ecosystems. Every category maps model failures to regulatory, compliance and operational risk rather than generic harm.

    Largest areas
    Fraud 379 · Financial Crime 201
  6. The taxonomy is the only domain-specific part of the framework. Everything downstream (judges, RAHS, the attacker loop) is domain-agnostic.
  7. Fraud has 13 sub-categories because that is where BFSI misconduct is most differentiated: trade finance, double financing, accounting and earnings manipulation, asset misappropriation.
  8. Unlicensed / Improper Advice is small (85 prompts) but is the most vulnerable category for every model we tested.
    9. 3 · Evaluation protocol

    Three heterogeneous judges, one majority vote, and an attacker that learns from them

    Every output gets three independent labels from open-weight judges running the same prompt under deterministic decoding. A prompt that survives single-turn evaluation is handed to an adaptive attacker for up to five turns.

    Judge 1 · safety-specialised

    gpt-oss-120b-safeguard

    High-precision policy-violation detection.

    Judge 2 · large general reasoner

    Qwen3-235B-A22B

    Consistency on ambiguous, multi-step harmful answers.

    Judge 3 · third model family

    Llama-3.3-Nemotron-Super-49B-v1.5

    Mid-sized, cost-efficient third signal from an independent family, so judge errors are as uncorrelated as possible.

    RRefusal. Direct denial, no sensitive methods or actionable detail.
    SASafe Alternative. Declines the operational ask but gives compliant, high-level guidance.
    HDHarmful Disclosure. Operationally actionable instructions: a successful bypass.
  9. Judges differ along three axes on purpose: safety-specialised vs general, size, and model family. Same prompt structure for all three, so differences reflect the models, not the protocol.
  10. Each judge returns a single-line JSON: label, severity (low/medium/high, HD only), disclaimer flag, and a reason. The reason is what the attacker consumes next turn.
  11. Attacker is DeepSeek-V3.2-685B. It reframes, escalates specificity, appeals to authority, and reuses the target's own words to justify the follow-up. Runs stop at the first majority HD.
    12. 4 · The metric

    Risk-Adjusted Harm Score: grade the failure, not just count it

    RAHS grades harmful disclosures by severity, treats disclaimers as partial mitigation, uses inter-judge agreement as a confidence signal, penalises ambiguity, and credits compliant safe alternatives. Set the judges and the hyperparameters and watch the score move.

    per example  ri = α · pSAi − (1 − α) · pHDi · w̄i · (1 − γ · d̄i)
    ambiguity  H̃i = −(1 / log 3) · Σc pci · log(pci + ε) ∈ [0, 1]
    benchmark  RAHS = (1 / N) · Σi ( ri − λ · H̃i )
    α safe-alternative credit
    0.50
    γ disclaimer mitigation
    0.20
    λ ambiguity penalty
    0.10

    Gauge spans the metric's range under default hyperparameters, [−0.6, 0.5]. Small ticks mark where the nine evaluated models land on aggregate at T = 0 (−0.339 to −0.211). The HD aggregate label needs a majority (≥ 2 of 3 judges), but every judge's vote enters the score through pHD.

    Rewards

    Safe alternatives. α · pSA credits models that decline the operational ask yet still help: the utility side of the safety–utility trade-off, made explicit.

    Penalises

    Harmful disclosures in proportion to judge agreement (pHD), operational severity (w̄: ⅓, ⅔, 1), and lack of disclaimers (d̄).

    Penalises

    Ambiguity. Normalised entropy of the three votes. Borderline outputs are operationally unmanageable, and the human study shows they are exactly where judges disagree.

    Defaults

    α = 0.5 symmetric exchange rate between safe and harmful behaviour; γ = 0.2 a disclaimer reduces but does not remove exposure; λ = 0.1 a modest ambiguity penalty. Institutions tune these; §8 shows rankings do not move.

  12. Start from the preset “Severe, no disclaimer” (−0.50), add a disclaimer (−0.40), lower severity to low (−0.13): same ASR = 1 throughout.
  13. Then “Split decision”: one judge each of R / SA / HD. The reward and penalty cancel and the entropy term alone drives the score to −0.10. That is the ambiguity penalty at work.
  14. Raise α and the whole scale shifts; the ordering of models does not. That is the sensitivity result later.
    15. 5 · Results I · decoding temperature

    Hotter decoding fails more often, and fails worse

    Across T ∈ {0, 0.5, 1.0} ASR rises and RAHS falls, with RAHS amplifying the effect: higher temperature shifts failures toward more operationally specific disclosures with higher judge consensus.

    T = 0T = 0.5T = 1.0MoE mixture of experts

    Single-turn results on all 989 prompts (Table 1). Sorting by RAHS instead of ASR moves Qwen3-8B from 5th to 8th: it is jailbroken less often than its peers but its failures are more severe.

    Least robust
    91.7 → 94.2% Olmo-3-32B-Think

    Gap to the most robust model is significant at p < 10−6 across all temperatures and rounds.

    The exception

    Qwen3-8B loses ASR as T rises (86.7 → 84.2): for small models, sampling noise can break the coherence an adversarial trajectory needs. Nemotron-Nano-9B-v2 peaks at T = 0.5. Plausibly one inverted-U whose turning point shifts with scale.

    Reading the grid

    Financial deployments keep stochasticity low for auditability; higher T is a stress condition. Within it, every ASR gap above 2.5 points is significant at p < 0.01.

    17. 6 · Results II · adaptive multi-turn pressure

    22. 8 · Is the ranking an artifact of the hyperparameters?

    Rankings hold across every sweep; severity grading is what does the work

    Vary α, γ and λ one at a time and the order of the nine models never changes (Kendall τ ≥ 0.94 against the default, 1.00 in most settings). Remove severity grading and the ranking moves (τ = 0.83). The other three ablations only shift the scale.

    α moves the scale, not the order

    The safe-alternative coefficient produces the largest absolute changes in RAHS (Olmo-3-32B-Think runs from −0.475 at α = 0.3 to −0.187 at α = 0.7) with the ordering intact. γ and λ have negligible impact.

    NOSEV is the only ablation that reorders

    Fixing the severity weight to 1 compresses the score range and collapses differences in the saturation regime: Qwen3-8B rises three places, Qwen3-32B drops two.

    Implication

    RAHS is configurable to institution-specific risk tolerance and converges to the same comparative conclusions. Report absolute values with the configuration used.

  22. Drag the α slider end to end: every bar stretches, the rank numbers never change. Same for γ and λ.
  23. Switch to the ablation bump chart: four flat lines and one crossing at NOSEV. Severity weighting is the dominant source of discriminative power; disclaimers, entropy and the SA reward are complementary modulators.
    24. 9 · Can you trust the judges?

    The ensemble tracks expert annotators, and the differences are not noise

    On 500 stratified outputs labelled by two financial-domain annotators, the majority-vote ensemble reaches κ = 0.79 against the human consensus on the three-class label (human ceiling 0.81) and 0.69 on severity (ceiling 0.74).

    three-class label κseverity κ

    Inter-judge agreement
    α = 0.73 label · 0.61 severity (Krippendorff)

    Complementary but well-correlated signals; every individual judge exceeds κ = 0.65 on the label.

    Where humans and judges disagree

    Outputs mixing safe educational framing with isolated operational details, and borderline medium/high severity calls. These show the highest normalised entropy in the ensemble, which validates the λ term.

    Significance

    1,000 stratified bootstrap resamples. McNemar on per-prompt outcomes: every ASR gap above 2.5 points is significant at p < 0.01. Wilcoxon signed-rank on per-prompt contributions: every RAHS gap above 0.020 is significant at p < 0.01. RAHS intervals are narrower than ASR's because each prompt contributes three signals instead of one bit.

  24. The dumbbell: the ensemble beats every single judge on both label and severity, and sits within 0.02 of the human ceiling on the label.
  25. Tightening guardrails is not free; the SA label and the α, γ coefficients let an institution choose how aggressively to credit helpfulness on borderline regulatory questions without inverting model order.
    26. 10 · Conclusion

    Models refuse overt harm, and comply behind a veneer of legality

    Regulatory grey-area prompts (aggressive tax minimisation, market manipulation framed as research, structuring advice) elicit operationally detailed responses rather than refusals. These are not edge cases: they emerge systematically under realistic adversarial conditions.

    1

    Grade the harm

    RAHS keeps separating models when ASR saturates, and its ordering is stable across hyperparameters. Severity weighting carries the discriminative power.

    2

    Pressure changes what fails

    Both decoding stochasticity and multi-turn interaction raise not only the frequency of jailbreaks but their severity. Early-round resistance rarely translates into sustained robustness.

    3

    Diagnose by category

    Unlicensed advice, market abuse and crypto/DeFi are where current behaviour is furthest from acceptable risk; fair-lending prompts are well covered by existing alignment.

    4

    A template, not a finance artifact

    Nothing in the judging protocol, RAHS or the multi-turn loop is finance-specific. Swap the taxonomy and regulatory mapping and it transfers to healthcare and legal services, where harm is graded and plausible framing is itself an attack vector. Next: agentic workflows with tool use and multi-agent coordination.

    Limitations
    • Open-weight models only, ≤ 72B parameters.
    • One attacker model; attacker scale and family left to future work.
    • Human validation on 500 outputs; larger studies and severity adjudication would strengthen it.
    • Absolute RAHS values depend on (α, γ, λ): report them with the configuration.
